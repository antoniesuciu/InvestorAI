# Phase 1 workstream: News and financial intelligence

Research date and source access date: **2026-10-27**. Status: recommendations for review, not integrations. This workstream used official documentation and terms, including a successful live Firecrawl retrieval of SEC developer resources. No account was created, credentialed API tested, dependency installed, or application implemented. Web documentation retrieval is not evidence of production uptime or dataset completeness.

## Findings and proposed role

Use official company disclosures and statistical releases as the evidence base, with GDELT's open metadata as a discovery layer. Keep article discovery separate from permission to collect, retain, redistribute, or analyze publishers' full text. No source examined establishes a complete, unrestricted, survivorship-free historical archive of global financial news at zero cost.

This is an architectural recommendation: material claims discovered through secondary news should be checked against filings, issuer releases, official economic releases, or several independently reporting publishers. A syndicated article copied to multiple domains is one underlying report, not independent corroboration.

### GDELT historical-search documentation requires special care

The 2017 DOC reference describes a rolling three-month horizon. A newer official May 2018 announcement says January 1, 2017 became a fixed search start; non-timeline outputs consider the last three months **of the requested window**, allowing narrower older windows. Thus “DOC only covers the last three calendar months” is not a defensible blanket conclusion from the original documentation alone. The endpoint's actual current historical behavior still needs a small live validation before being promised to a user. An attempted browser retrieval of a 2017 sample query was inaccessible through the research tool; this does not establish whether the provider API itself accepts or rejects that request. [2018 update](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/doc-2-0-updates-1-5-year-searching-and-updated-mobile-interface/).

Keep these distinct: Event history from 1979, GKG/2.0 metadata history, DOC search behavior, and licensed publisher full text. An old URL in a metadata file is not a preserved historical article or evidence that today's page equals its original version. The separately branded `gdeltcloud.com` is not the raw Project API recommendation; do not substitute a metered hosted service for the free endpoints.

The Project's reviewed terms grant broad use and redistribution of its datasets and do not state an AI-specific prohibition or a storage deadline. This supports retaining the released metadata with attribution; it does not establish permission to train models on or archive linked publishers' full articles. Treat model training and full-text analysis as separate rights questions. No broader AI-rights conclusion was established.

## Earnings and competitor intelligence

Monitor filing events and issuer announcements together. SEC's investor guide explains that 8-K Item 2.02 often contains an earnings release as an exhibit before full periodic statements, while Item 3.01 addresses delisting/listing-compliance notices. The trigger date and filing availability time can differ. [SEC investor guide to 8-K](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/how-read-8).

For competitor/industry research, use 10-K/10-Q business descriptions, risks, MD&A and material contracts, compare issuer releases and GDELT entity/topic candidates, and check relevant public agencies. Management commentary remains management commentary. [SEC guide to annual/quarterly reports](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/how-read).

Public procurement notices can later add industry demand signals: EU TED offers sector RSS, read-only search operations and daily/monthly XML downloads without signing in. It is a targeted procurement source, not proof of realized company revenue; entity matching and individual reuse conditions need separate review. [TED official data-reuse and access guide](https://ted.europa.eu/en/help/data-reuse).

No completely free, licensed, comprehensive global historical earnings-call transcript archive or historical analyst-consensus calendar was established here. Public issuer presentations/webcasts can provide selective evidence, with transcription/media permissions considered individually. Issuer guidance is not analyst consensus, and a later reported surprise cannot reconstruct the forecast available before earnings.

## Proposed monitoring and evidence model

This is conceptual design for later review, not implemented behavior:

1. Maintain a small allowlist of sources with documented purpose, free-access proof, allowed fields, retention, attribution and review date. Disable sources when terms or access become unsuitable; no paid fallback.
2. Use scheduled release calendars to prioritize polling, with timezone-aware UTC conversion and calendar revision history. Keep provider ceilings independent from our more conservative polling budget.
3. Identify filings by CIK/accession and news by provider ID/canonical URL. Resolve company aliases separately from ticker strings. Deduplicate syndication before interpreting coverage counts.
4. Keep `published_at`, `provider_first_seen_at`, `retrieved_at`, and `available_to_research_at` separately, including timestamp provenance/uncertainty. Store version hashes and raw content only where retention is permitted. Never fabricate a timestamp from a reporting period.
5. Retain allowed metadata and provenance for articles whose full text cannot be stored. Link to the source; flag inability to reproduce full-text analysis. A hash can verify bytes but cannot recover missing content.
6. Validate a material alert against original disclosures and record contradictory evidence. Store evidence and model interpretation separately, including issuer bias, source uncertainty and untrusted-input handling.
7. Detect stale feeds, missing expected releases, duplicated records, 429/403 responses and schema drift. Backoff, bound retries, and surface gaps; no claim of guaranteed detection latency.

## Phase 2 research-to-implementation handoff

Begin with a limited US-company watchlist plus official US/euro-area release feeds, then assess selective GDELT metadata. Before implementing historical news signals, validate a small, versioned historical sample for source availability, duplicate stories, changed pages, entity-linking errors and timestamp semantics. Record recall gaps; benchmark only against the observable corpus. Defer commercial-content aggregators until their intended-use and retention rights are established without payment.

Important independent checks for the coordinating reviewer: NewsAPI's internal-production exclusion; Guardian's explicit commercial-mining restriction; GDELT's later history update versus its original reference; SEC's access ceiling; and the difference between article metadata rights and publisher-text rights. These are the findings most likely to change the proposed stack.
